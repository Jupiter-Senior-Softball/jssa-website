The Board Members Portal

A plain-English guide to every tool in the portal and how to use it. No computer experience required — if you can fill out a form and click a button, you can run the league's website and its record-keeping.

The Board Portal is the private, password-protected control room for everything the JSSA runs online: the website, the membership and registration records, the pickup games, and the fall and winter leagues. Anything you post to the website appears within about a minute. This guide walks through every section, what it controls, and the handful of rules worth remembering.

  2. Click Board Portal in the menu at the top of the page. (It's also in the Quick Links list on the homepage, as "Board Members Portal.")

3. Make an announcement (the banner across the top)

This is the fastest tool in the portal and the one you'll use most. Whatever you post here shows up in the colored strip at the very top of every page of the website.

The form, field by field

  • What kind of update? — Game-day / weather for rainouts and field conditions, or Announcement for everything else. When a weather notice is live it takes priority in the banner.
  • Message — keep it short; it has to fit in one banner line. Example: "No games today — fields are wet from overnight rain. Check back tomorrow."
  • Link text (optional) — a short phrase like "Sign up here" that becomes the clickable part. The message itself stays plain text.
  • Link URL (optional) — the web address that phrase points to. Leave both link boxes blank if you don't need a link.
  • Your name — so the board knows who posted it.

Then click Post update. It's on the website within about a minute.

Managing what's already posted

Scroll down to Posted updates and you'll see everything you've ever posted, with its status: ● Live (showing on the website) or ○ Off (saved but hidden). Each row has three buttons:

Button What it does
EditFix a typo or reword the message. Click Save when you're done.
Turn off / Turn onThis is the one you want for a rainout notice — turn it off when games are back to normal, and it's ready to reuse next time.
DeleteRemoves it for good. It asks you to confirm first.
Rule of thumb: "Turn off" beats "Delete." A rainout notice you turn off can be turned back on the next rainy Monday in one click. A deleted one has to be retyped.

4. Blackboard posts (the cards on the homepage)

The Blackboard is the set of announcement cards on the homepage — community nights, sponsor shout-outs, league news. Bigger and more permanent than a banner notice.

What goes on a card

  • Headline — the big line. "Free scoop at Village Scoop Shack"
  • Small label above the headline (optional) — "Community Night · Tuesday, July 7"
  • Message — the body of the announcement.
  • Button link + button text (optional) — adds a clickable button, e.g. "Visit website."
  • Photo (optional) — paste a Google Drive share link (see the box below). No photo is fine; you get a clean text-only card.
  • Photo side — picture on the left or the right.
  • Sign-off (optional) — "— John & Toni Cariero"

Cards show top-to-bottom by their Order number, lowest first, and the first one is displayed a little larger as the featured card. Same Edit / Turn off / Delete buttons as the banner announcements.

How to add a photo anywhere in the portal. Photos live in Google Drive, not on the website. In Drive: right-click the photo → Share → "Anyone with the link" → Copy link, then paste that link into the Photo box. If you skip the "Anyone with the link" step the picture won't show up — that's the single most common snag.

5. People & Recognition

Four buttons, all working the same way: a form at the top to add or edit, and a list underneath with Edit / Turn off / Delete on every row. Changes appear on the site in about a minute.

In Memoriam

Add a brother to the In Memoriam page: Name, Month & year, and an optional photo. The names already built into the page stay exactly as they are — anything you add joins them.

Hall of Fame

Add an inductee: Year, Name, an optional tribute (write as much as you like — leave a blank line between paragraphs and each becomes its own paragraph), and an optional photo. New inductees appear above the ones already on the page, newest first.

Board of Directors

Manage the board list on the homepage: Name, Role (Commissioner, Vice Commissioner, Treasurer, Director…), and Division for directors, which adds the colored dot and "· Red / White / Blue" after their name. The current board is already loaded, so this is mostly editing an existing row when someone changes seats. Officers with no division usually come first, then Red, White, Blue — controlled by the Order number.

Sponsors

Manage the sponsor tiles on the homepage: Business name, optional tagline, website, phone (shows when there's no website), and an optional logo (Google Drive link, same as photos). No logo? The sponsor automatically gets a clean navy initials tile — it still looks sharp. When a sponsor doesn't renew, just turn them off or delete the row.

6. Communications

Email Members

One-click emailing to the whole league, split by division. Pick a group and the portal does the address-gathering for you.

  1. Click a group: RED Division, WHITE Division, BLUE Division, or Board of Directors. Each button shows how many addresses it holds.
  2. Type a subject if you like (optional).
  3. Click ✉ Open in my email — your normal email program opens with everyone already filled in.
  4. Type your message and hit Send.

Three things to know

  • It sends from your own email account — the one you normally use on that device. The portal doesn't send anything itself; it just addresses the message for you.
  • Everyone goes in BCC, so no member ever sees anyone else's address.
  • If your email app doesn't open (some apps limit how many addresses fit in one link), click Copy email addresses instead, start a new email yourself, and paste them into the BCC field.

Click "Show who's included" to see the full list of names. Anyone without an email address on file is flagged in red so you know who's missing — that's a nudge to get their address into the member list.

Email Send Counter

Gmail limits how many people one account can email in a day (roughly 100). This page shows, for each league email account, how many sends are left today, how many messages went out, and how many people were reached. It refreshes on its own every minute, and the card turns amber and then red as an account gets close to its limit.

A group email in BCC counts as one message but reaches many people — the "sends left" number counts people, because that's what Gmail actually limits. If you're about to email a big division, glance here first.

7. Fall and Winter League Organizational Tools

Everything needed to stand up a league season and keep it current. Two buttons.

Templates for Teams, Schedules and Rosters

The master template for building a season: the team lists, the game schedule, and the division rosters. Start a new season by working from this template rather than from a blank sheet — that's what "Required for Website Uploads" means on the button. The website expects this exact layout, so a season built in this format loads cleanly onto the public Schedule, Teams, Rosters and Standings pages.

The formatting is yours to pretty up; the column headings are what the website actually looks for. On the schedule side it expects Date, Time, Field, Home Team, Away Team, Division, Score Home, Score Away and Status. On the roster side it expects First Name, Last Name, Division and Position. Add as many rows as you need — just don't rename the heading row, or the website won't recognize the columns.

League Game Scores

The score-entry screen. This is a page of the portal itself, not a spreadsheet:

  1. Pick a division. Each one shows how many games still need a score — a green zero means you're all caught up.
  2. You get that division's games with the ones still needing a score at the top, most recently played first. Already-scored games drop to the bottom but stay editable.
  3. Type both scores and click Save score.

The score writes straight into the Schedule sheet, and the Results and Standings update automatically within a couple of minutes — you never edit standings by hand. Got one wrong? Open the same game and click Update.

8. Pickup Game Management

The pickup games run three times a week — Monday, Wednesday and Friday — and are organized automatically. Players sign up through the player portal, the system sorts them into balanced teams for each game date, emails the captains, and publishes the rosters. These two buttons are where the board watches over that.

Pickup Player List

The master list of pickup players and their sign-ups — who's in the pool, their contact details, and which upcoming game dates each player has said yes to. This is the sheet the assignment system reads when it builds teams, so it's the place to look when someone says "I signed up and I'm not on the roster." Check that the player is on the list and that their date is marked.

JSSA Pickup Game Rosters

The teams the system produced for each game day — who's playing, on which team, at which field. This is what gets shared with the captains and the players. Use it the morning of a game to see the final lineups, or after the fact to see who actually played.

These two run themselves. The sign-ups, the team balancing, the captain emails and the roster publishing all happen on a schedule without anyone pressing a button. Read them freely; be careful about hand-editing them, because the automation may overwrite a manual change on its next run. If something looks wrong, tell Tom rather than fixing it in the sheet.

9. Registrations & Membership

The league's membership and money records. Five buttons, and four of them open different tabs of the same registration spreadsheet — so if one looks familiar, that's why.

Registration Dashboard

Start here. The at-a-glance summary of the season, rebuilt automatically every hour (it stamps the time it last refreshed, so you always know how current it is). It shows:

  • Paying Members Registered — the head count
  • Total Expected, Total Collected, Still Outstanding — the money, in dollars
  • Paid in Full and Still Owe — how many members are square and how many aren't
  • Guest Passes Issued — counted separately, not included in the dollar totals

This is the tab to open before a board meeting. You can safely re-format this tab by hand — the hourly rebuild only refreshes the numbers.

Completed Registration Submissions

The line-by-line record of everyone who has finished registering: registration ID, name, date of birth, email, phone, preferred position, shirt size, season, amount due, amount paid, balance and payment status. This is the detail behind the dashboard's numbers — the place to look up one specific person, or to see who still owes.

Guest Pass Registrations

Everyone who has taken a two-week guest pass — the prospective members trying us out. Worth a look every couple of weeks: a guest whose two weeks are up is exactly who to invite to join as a full member.

Member List

The JSSA master membership list — the league's roll of who belongs, with their contact information. This is the record of record; the website's rosters and the division email lists ultimately trace back to it. If a member's email bounces or their phone number changed, this is where the correction belongs.

Google Forms

The Drive folder holding the actual sign-up forms members fill out — new member registration, renewals, the guest pass, and the 85+5 membership. Open this when you need the link to send someone, or when the board wants to change a question on a form. A submitted form flows automatically into the registration spreadsheet and the tabs above; nobody re-types anything.

Handle with care. These sheets hold members' personal information — dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, and payment status — and the automation reads them. Look all you want, but don't delete rows, re-order columns, or rename tabs. If something needs correcting, change the one cell that's wrong, or ask Tom.

10. Player Directory

The portal also has a member contact page — name, division, email and phone, with a search box at the top. Type any part of a name or a division to filter it instantly. It's handy when you just need to reach one member and don't want to open a spreadsheet.

It isn't on the dashboard as a button right now; you get to it by going to jupiterseniorsoftball.com/admin/directory while signed in. (If the board would find it useful as a button, it's a one-row addition — see the next section.) This is board-only information; it never appears anywhere on the public website.

11. Site stats

Near the bottom of the dashboard is a Homepage visits counter — a running total of how many times people have opened the JSSA homepage. Nice for sponsor conversations.

12. Adding or changing the portal's buttons

Sections 7, 8 and 9 aren't built into the website — they're rows in a spreadsheet, which means the board can add, rename, hide or regroup any of those buttons without asking anyone to change the site.

Open the JSSA Website Control Sheet, go to the tab named "Board Portal Links", and add a row with four things:

Section Button name Link Show?
Registrations & Membership Completed Registration Submissions https://docs.google.com/… Yes
  • Section is the heading the button appears under. Type an existing heading exactly to add the button to that group, or invent a new heading to start a new group.
  • Button name is what the board will read on the tile — write it the way you'd say it out loud.
  • Link is the web address it opens (a Google Sheet, a Drive folder, a form, or a page of the website).
  • Show? set to Yes puts it on the portal. Change it to No to hide the button without losing the row.

The new button appears on the portal automatically within a minute or so. Nothing else to do, and nothing to break — a bad link just means a button that goes nowhere until you fix the cell.

13. Seven rules worth remembering

  1. You can't break the website. Nothing in the portal can take the site down. The worst case of a mistake is a wrong-looking card that you fix or turn off a minute later.
  2. The spreadsheets deserve more care than the website. Website mistakes are undone in a click; deleting a row of member or registration data is not. Read freely, edit narrowly.
  3. Changes take about a minute. If you don't see your change right away, wait a moment and refresh the page before re-posting it.
  4. "Turn off" is your friend. Hide things instead of deleting them; they're one click from coming back.
  5. Delete asks first, then it's gone. If a box pops up asking "are you sure," that's your last chance to say no.
  6. Photos come from Google Drive, and the share setting must be "Anyone with the link."
  7. Keep the password inside the board. Everyone signs into the same portal, so put your name on announcements — that's how we know who posted what.
